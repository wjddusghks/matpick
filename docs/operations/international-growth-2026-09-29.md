# International growth experiment — 2026-09-29

This 14-day plan runs from 2026-09-29 through 2026-10-12 within the existing five-channel cadence: two Korean and two English posts per channel each day, beginning at 11:00 KST and publishing immediately after review. It adds no schedules, channels, DMs, unsolicited comments, or ad spend.

The working hypothesis is that specific traveler decisions (neighborhood or station + food + budget) will produce more useful attention than generic how-to or logo-heavy cards. This is not a proven diagnosis of low views: the account is fresh and there are too few published days for a causal claim.

## Execution

- Verify restaurant name, exact branch, menu, price, quantity, address, source URL, and check date before drafting. If a fact is unavailable, hold the draft and record the missing item.
- Make English image text English-only. Add a plain-English food gloss and the Korean food name where useful. Keep the accurate Matpick explanation that the service helps discover restaurants from Korean-language YouTube food channels and TV food shows.
- Use original 3–5 slide Instagram comparisons designed for saves. Use Pinterest search-first evergreen pins with relevant title/description keywords, mobile-readable visuals, and a deeplink where supported. Use a concise native Threads question or decision criterion, not a copied ad. Use Naver titles and openings that match a concrete query.
- Add per-post UTM fields when the destination supports links: source, medium, campaign `international_20260929_14d`, content identifier, and language. Instagram profile links may be mentioned, but captions must not claim a clickable link.
- Record impressions, reach, saves, profile visits, link clicks, and site visits separately at 24 hours and 7 days. Record unavailable metrics as null. Compare matched channel, language, format, and topic cohorts; report association only.

## Six English angle examples

These are creative angles only. They are not claims that any named restaurant is verified or recommended. A future post may use one only after its restaurant facts are checked.

1. **“Near [station]: one Korean soup, one noodle option, one budget.”** Compare two or three verified choices by walking context, dish type, and KRW range.
2. **“First meal after landing in [neighborhood].”** Show a verified menu choice under a stated budget, with Korean name, food gloss, branch, and check date.
3. **“Solo dinner in [district]: what one order actually buys.”** Compare serving quantity and price for verified menu items; avoid claiming portion satisfaction.
4. **“Rainy-day [food] near [station].”** Use a verified indoor or location fact only when sourced, then compare menu and budget.
5. **“Two ways to order [dish] in [neighborhood].”** Explain verified menu variants and Korean terms, with no invented best-of ranking.
6. **“[District] food stop for a group of three.”** Compare verified item combinations and total KRW math, clearly labeling assumptions about the order.

At the end of day 14, retain the raw post-level windows and matched cohorts. Do not call the experiment successful from impressions alone or convert link clicks/site visits into restaurant visits.
